UX Planning

Stage 1: Brand Logic & Information Architecture

Defining Evergreen Architecture

Shifted away from an outdated "event-driven" structure. By auditing the organization's core values, we reorganized the site into timeless "Ministry" and "Core Events" categories, significantly reducing future maintenance debt.

Mapping a Frictionless Journey

For high-friction conversion goals like "Marriage Counseling," we redesigned the user flow. We integrated psychological safety markers (e.g., spatial photos, FAQs) and guided users smoothly into a soft-landing LINE OA inquiry point.

Wireframing for Stakeholder Consensus

Navigating multiple stakeholders, I utilized low-fi wireframes early on to ground discussions. This established a solid foundation for brand identity and allowed us to pivot agilely during the subsequent PR crisis.

Design Iterations

Adapting design for trust, evergreen maintenance, and crisis control

Iteration 1: From "Event-Driven" to "Value-Driven" Content

The Content Decay Problem
The site described specific past events and groups that no longer existed, creating a disconnect with current reality and confusing visitors.
Evergreen Re-categorization
Categorized activities under a broader "Fellowship" section. Used first-person, conversational copywriting to highlight shared values rather than fleeting dates, building a proactive dialogue with the visitor.

Iteration 2: Building Trust for High-Friction Services

Hesitation to Convert
The Marriage Counseling page had good organic SEO, but the outdated UI and lack of counselor philosophies made hesitant users afraid to book a session.
Progressive Disclosure for Psychological Safety
Modernized the page with photos of the actual counseling space. I introduced an FAQ section and placed deep-dive counseling philosophies on a secondary page, keeping the main landing page intuitive while providing depth for those who need it.

Iteration 3: Agile Design for Crisis Communication

The Pivot (PR Crisis)
During development, the organization faced malicious online rumors. The stakeholders urgently needed a way to clarify their theological background and financial integrity.
Transparency Section Design
I rapidly pivoted the "About Us" page. Replaced personal stories with a "Theological Foundations" layout and designed a "Transparency Section" featuring External CPA Audits and Financial Reports. I also added a marquee showcasing international branches to visually reinforce global credibility.
